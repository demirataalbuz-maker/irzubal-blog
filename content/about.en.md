## Why this blog exists

In AI security, the best textbook is real incidents. Here I take publicly disclosed AI security incidents and read them from both sides: what the attacker saw, what the defence missed, and what I would have done. Every analysis follows the same six steps and is mapped to the OWASP LLM Top 10 and MITRE ATLAS, so over time the posts become a searchable incident archive.

Analyses rely only on public sources, and every claim is sourced at the end of the post.

## What I build

In my own lab, with local models (Ollama, LM Studio), I build both offensive and defensive tools:

- [Offensive AI Labs](https://github.com/demirataalbuz-maker/offensive-ai-labs): a local red-team harness for agent tool abuse, ML evasion and model-file supply chain experiments.
- [Agent Reliability Test Bed](https://github.com/demirataalbuz-maker/ajan-guvenilirlik-test-ortami): a closed sandbox that measures tool-calling agents against indirect prompt injection, excessive agency (OWASP LLM06) and data leakage scenarios.
- [LLM Audit Layer](https://github.com/demirataalbuz-maker/llm-denetim-katmani): a FastAPI proxy between user and model that blocks injection attempts and masks Turkish personal data (KVKK).
- [TürkBench](https://github.com/demirataalbuz-maker/turkbench): a Turkish LLM reliability benchmark; 6 sets, 52 scenarios, rule-based scoring.
